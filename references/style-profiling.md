# Style profiling from photo / wardrobe / inspiration folders

## 1. Read the three source folders

Typical user layout on the desktop:

| Folder | Contents | What to extract |
|---|---|---|
| `个人照片/` | Real photos of the user | Face shape, hairstyle, skin tone, body type, height impression, colours actually worn |
| `我的衣柜/` | Screenshots of Taobao/Tmall product pages (saved or purchased) | Item category, colour, material, listed price, style vibe |
| `穿搭灵感库/` | Saved street-style / brand lookbook images | Styling tricks only: colour blocking, layering, how black shoes/bags close a look |

Read 4–6 personal photos, 8–15 wardrobe screenshots, 3–5 inspiration images. Do not read the whole folder — sample evenly by filename.

## 2. Build the style profile

Derive, in this order:

1. **Dominant palette** — count the colours that actually recur in her photos. This overrides any assumption from a single photo.
2. **Silhouette formula** — the repeated construction across her photos (e.g. high waist + tucked hem + mid-calf skirt).
3. **Signature markers** — the details that make her recognisable (hair length, lip colour, earrings, a specific bag).
4. **Secondary branches** — styles present in her wardrobe screenshots but not in her photos. Treat these as mix-in options, not the main axis.

Write it as a short block the user can argue with, not a horoscope. Point at evidence ("照片里三套都有橄榄绿" beats "你适合自然色系").

## 3. Wardrobe inventory rules

Wardrobe screenshots are **shopping intent, not ownership**. Tag every item with one of three sources:

| badge | source value | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 已有 | `owned` | Visible in her personal photos — she definitely owns it |
| 衣柜收藏 | `cart` | From a Taobao screenshot; include the price shown at capture time |
| 建议补 | `buy` | Not present anywhere; the gap that makes the outfit work |

Aim for 5 looks where at least 3 items per look are `owned` or `cart`. A look that is 100% `buy` is a shopping list, not a lookbook.

## 4. Composing the 5 looks

Cover distinct occasions rather than five variations of one idea, for example: commute, casual day, statement/monochrome, vintage/literary, date night.

Each look needs:
- a name built from its two leading pieces
- an occasion tag
- one line of **why it suits this body** (which line it extends, what it hides)
- 4–5 items with a hard fit requirement ("袖长到腕骨", "裤脚不堆布") — fit beats brand

## 5. Colour discipline

Keep the palette to 3 colours per look: one dominant cream/oat, one grounding tone (black, brown, olive), one accent (Klein blue, true red). If the wardrobe already leans cream/oat/brown, use the accent as the memory point and let black close the look (shoes + bag).

## 6. Search keywords

Turn every item into a search query that encodes fit, not just category:

- weak: `西装裤`
- strong: `高腰 九分 直筒 西装裤 女 垂感 免烫`

Always append 女 and the material/behaviour words that actually change the garment. The link builder URL-encodes these automatically.
